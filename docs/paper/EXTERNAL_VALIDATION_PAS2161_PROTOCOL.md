# Prospective External Engineering Validation Protocol

## ORED Governance Compiler × PAS 2161 Road-Condition-Monitoring Approval Process

**Protocol version:** 1.0  
**Freeze date:** 28 September 2026  
**Status:** Prospective; external outcomes not yet used for model or rule tuning  
**DTEP baseline:** v2.4 ORED Governance Compiler  
**External operator:** UK Department for Transport (DfT) / Transport Research Laboratory (TRL)

## 1. Objective

This protocol tests whether the Object–Relation–Evidence–Decision (ORED) governance architecture remains sufficient when applied to an engineering approval workflow that was designed and operated independently of DTEP.

The validation is intentionally minimal. It does not add a third internal demonstration case and does not seek broad statistical generalization. It asks whether one complete, externally produced engineering approval path can be represented, evaluated, invalidated, and traced using the four existing ORED layers without adding a new architecture layer or a PAS2161-specific branch to the governance core.

The external validity question is:

> Can a frozen ORED implementation map an independently designed engineering approval process from artifacts and relationships to admissible evidence and a governed decision, and can it fail closed when the external evidence chain becomes incomplete, mismatched, or stale?

## 2. Why PAS 2161 is an appropriate external holdout

The PAS 2161 road-condition-monitoring (RCM) approval process is suitable for five reasons.

First, it is organizationally independent. The standard and demonstration process are operated by DfT and TRL, not by the DTEP team.

Second, it is technically different from CASE-01 and CASE-02. The external process concerns road-condition-monitoring technologies, engineer benchmark surveys, repeatability, accuracy metrics, quality-assurance information, and approval for national road-condition reporting.

Third, it has an explicit evidence-to-decision structure. The 2025 demonstration assessed RCM technologies against engineer and provider benchmarks and repeat-route data using predefined performance tests. Required thresholds and additional non-required tests were combined into a demonstrated/not-demonstrated decision. Nine technologies were reported as demonstrated and approved for network use.

Fourth, the approval has a real lifecycle condition. The 2025 final report states that demonstrated status applies only while the provider continues to use the same methods and contractual arrangements that were in place during the trial. TRL's 2026 quality-assurance materials additionally request information on collection limitations, additional vehicles/devices, calibration and consistency, and invalid/low-confidence data. These requirements provide a genuine external analogue of evidence validity and requalification triggers.

Fifth, the 2026 process creates a prospective holdout. TRL's 2026 materials identify new trial networks in Cheshire West and Chester, Lincolnshire, and Nottinghamshire. The provider briefing schedules benchmark data for calibration on 2 October 2026 and calibrated provider submissions on 6 November 2026, while the FAQ states an intention to announce demonstrated/not-demonstrated results on 18 December 2026. This protocol is frozen on 28 September 2026, before those external outcomes are available for tuning.

## 3. Independence conditions

The external validation is valid only if all of the following conditions are met.

1. The engineering artifacts were generated by DfT, TRL, participating local authorities, or an RCM technology provider independently of DTEP.
2. The ORED layer definitions and core admission logic are frozen before the technology-level outcome used for validation is observed.
3. No PAS2161-specific conditional branch is added to the ORED resolver, evidence-admission engine, or decision compiler after outcome disclosure.
4. Domain-specific adapters may translate file/field names into generic ORED objects and relations, but they may not alter the meaning of the four layers.
5. The official external decision must come from TRL/DfT or a technology-level report/certificate produced through that process.
6. If technology-level metrics required for independent decision reconstruction are unavailable, the study will report a mapping-only result and will not claim external validation of decision reconstruction.

## 4. Frozen external workflow

### 4.1 External artifacts

The minimum expected artifact set for one technology is:

- technology identity and provider identity;
- 2026 trial-network definition for the three local-authority areas;
- technology main-route submission;
- technology repeat-route submissions;
- engineer benchmark dataset or official benchmark-derived assessment;
- provider benchmark data or official provider-benchmark assessment, where used;
- PAS 2161 quality-assurance form;
- gap/coverage report where applicable;
- official technology assessment report containing the metrics used in the demonstration decision;
- official demonstrated/not-demonstrated outcome; and
- certificate or approval record if demonstrated.

The benchmark datasets and route resources are published or distributed by TRL. Technology-level assessment reports may require provider consent or direct provision from TRL/provider.

### 4.2 External decision rule

The 2025 final report defines ten assessment tests based on six performance measures.

Engineer benchmark:

- Precision 0: required, threshold ≥60%;
- Precision 1: required, threshold ≥90%;
- Chi-squared: non-required, threshold ≤250;
- Macro Recall: non-required, threshold ≥40%.

Provider benchmark:

- Precision 0: required, threshold ≥60%;
- Precision 1: required, threshold ≥90%;
- Chi-squared: non-required, threshold ≤250;
- Macro Recall: non-required, threshold ≥40%.

Repeat data:

- Consistency — All Match: required, threshold ≥50%;
- Consistency — None Match: required, threshold ≤5.0%.

The 2025 rule requires all required tests to pass and at least two of the four non-required tests to pass. The 2026 FAQ indicates that the 2025 pass marks were expected to remain unchanged. For the external validation, the final 2026 criteria published by TRL/DfT will be frozen before technology outcomes are opened. If the official 2026 rule differs, the external decision compiler will use the published 2026 rule and record the difference as external process evolution rather than an ORED change.

## 5. Frozen ORED mapping

The mapping below is defined before the 2026 technology outcome is observed.

| ORED layer | PAS 2161 representation | Required contract |
|---|---|---|
| Object | Technology; provider; local-authority network; route/section; engineer benchmark; provider benchmark; technology submission; repeat submission; QA form; assessment report; certificate | Stable identity and type for every artifact that contributes to the decision |
| Relation | technology-surveys-route; benchmark-applies-to-route; repeat-is-repeat-of-main-survey; provider-submits-dataset; QA-belongs-to-technology; assessment-uses-benchmark; certificate-applies-to-technology/method | Typed endpoints; no cross-technology mixing; explicit benchmark and method identity |
| Evidence | Precision 0/1; chi-squared; Macro Recall; repeatability tests; QA status; gap/coverage evidence; official assessment values | Evidence admitted only if linked to the correct technology, route, benchmark, assessment version, and method state |
| Decision | demonstrated / not demonstrated; approval validity interval | All required tests plus the published optional-test rule; unresolved hard evidence defects fail closed |

This mapping is sufficient only if all decision-relevant PAS 2161 artifacts can be expressed without adding a fifth governance layer.

## 6. External validation endpoints

### 6.1 Primary endpoint: exact decision reconstruction

For one technology with a complete official assessment package, the frozen ORED mapping and frozen 2026 decision rule must reproduce the official TRL/DfT demonstrated/not-demonstrated outcome exactly.

The primary endpoint is binary:

[
Y_{mathrm{decision}} =
mathbb{I}[
widehat{D}_{mathrm{ORED}} = D_{mathrm{official}}
].
]

Success requires (Y_{mathrm{decision}}=1).

No probabilistic tolerance or partial credit is used because the external decision is categorical.

### 6.2 Architecture-sufficiency endpoint

The complete approval path must be represented using the existing four layers.

Success requires:

- 100% of decision-relevant external artifact classes assigned to one of the four layers;
- no new top-level governance layer;
- no PAS2161-specific branch in the core ORED resolver/admission/decision code;
- all external decision inputs traceable from the technology object to the final decision.

A domain adapter or vocabulary mapping is permitted. A new architectural contract dimension required only after seeing the outcome counts as a failure of the frozen architecture-sufficiency endpoint.

### 6.3 Fail-closed endpoint

Four negative controls will be applied to copies of the external artifact graph.

**NC1 — Missing benchmark relation.** Remove the relation between one assessment dataset and the engineer benchmark.

Expected result: the dependent evidence cannot enter the trusted set; the demonstration decision is blocked rather than inferred from remaining scores.

**NC2 — Cross-technology contamination.** Replace one technology's assessment or repeat dataset with an artifact carrying another technology identity.

Expected result: relation/evidence identity mismatch is detected before decision evaluation.

**NC3 — Assessment-version mismatch.** Attach metric values to a decision rule or assessment record with a non-matching protocol/criteria version.

Expected result: the evidence is inadmissible until the version relation is resolved.

**NC4 — Incomplete QA/method identity.** Remove the method or QA identity needed to establish that the approved configuration is the one being evaluated.

Expected result: approval use fails closed because the evidence-to-technology configuration cannot be established.

Success requires all four negative controls to be rejected before a demonstrated decision can be emitted.

### 6.4 Change-propagation endpoint

The change-propagation test uses the external approval condition that demonstrated status applies only while the same methods and contractual arrangements remain in place.

A frozen method-state fingerprint will be constructed from the technology/method identity and the decision-relevant QA fields available in the official assessment package. A change event is introduced by replacing the frozen method state with a different documented method/contract/QA state.

Expected propagation:

[
	ext{method-state change}
ightarrow
	ext{approval evidence stale}
ightarrow
	ext{trusted approval relation removed}
ightarrow
	ext{use of demonstrated status blocked}
ightarrow
	ext{reassessment required}.
]

Success requires a traceable stale reason and removal of the affected approval evidence without classifying the underlying ontology as corrupt.

If an actual provider change/reassessment becomes publicly documented, that real event will replace the controlled change. Otherwise the controlled perturbation will use the official QA/method fields while preserving the independently generated external artifact package.

## 7. Minimal sample and stopping rule

The minimum sample is one technology with:

1. an official technology-level assessment report containing all metrics necessary for the final decision;
2. a corresponding official demonstrated/not-demonstrated outcome;
3. sufficient method/QA identity to evaluate approval validity.

One complete technology is sufficient for the first external gate because the objective is architectural transport, not estimation of a population performance rate.

The validation stops after the first complete technology package has been processed through:

- exact decision reconstruction;
- four negative controls; and
- one change-propagation test.

Additional technologies may be reported as replication cases but are not required to declare the initial gate complete.

## 8. Data-access tiers

External validity claims depend on artifact availability.

### Tier A — Complete technology-level package

Available:

- technology-level official metrics;
- external outcome;
- method/QA identity.

Permitted claim: full external engineering validation of mapping, decision reconstruction, fail-closed behavior, and change propagation.

### Tier B — Official outcome + incomplete metrics

Available:

- external outcome/certificate;
- process and benchmark artifacts;
- no complete technology-level metric report.

Permitted claim: external structural mapping and change-governance validation only.

Not permitted: claim of independent decision reconstruction.

### Tier C — Public process documentation only

Available:

- PAS 2161 reports, routes, benchmark/calibration data, QA templates, criteria;
- no technology-level assessment package.

Permitted claim: prospective protocol feasibility only.

Not permitted: claim that ORED has passed external validation.

## 9. Leakage controls

The following information must remain unopened or unused for tuning until the relevant mapping and decision rule are frozen:

- the selected 2026 technology's final demonstrated/not-demonstrated result;
- its official final assessment report;
- provider commentary explaining borderline results;
- any post-result adjustments to the assessment criteria.

The Git commit containing this protocol and the external adapter schema will serve as the preregistration timestamp.

If the result becomes known inadvertently before the adapter/mapping commit is frozen, that technology cannot serve as the primary prospective holdout. Another technology or a later demonstration round must be selected.

## 10. Analysis outputs

The external validation will produce only four primary outputs.

| Output | Measure |
|---|---|
| Mapping completeness | decision-relevant artifact classes mapped / total required artifact classes |
| Decision reconstruction | exact ORED vs official demonstrated/not-demonstrated match |
| Negative-control rejection | rejected negative controls / 4 |
| Change propagation | expected stale → evidence removal → decision block trace reproduced: yes/no |

No composite score will be used. A failure in one endpoint will remain visible and will not be averaged away by success in another endpoint.

## 11. Success criterion

The full external gate passes only if:

1. the selected package is Tier A;
2. mapping completeness is 100%;
3. the official decision is reproduced exactly;
4. all four negative controls fail closed;
5. the change-propagation trace is reproduced; and
6. no new ORED layer or PAS2161-specific core governance branch is introduced.

If the artifacts are Tier B or Tier C, the completed subset will be reported, but the full external gate remains open.

## 12. Interpretation

A pass would support a narrow external claim: the Object–Relation–Evidence–Decision contracts are sufficient to reproduce one independently designed engineering approval workflow and its evidence-governance behavior.

A pass would not establish that ORED is universally sufficient across T&E organizations or regulatory regimes.

A failure will be classified before any architecture modification as one of:

- **contract failure:** a necessary external governance concept cannot be represented by the four layers;
- **observability failure:** the concept fits the architecture but required external artifacts are unavailable;
- **implementation failure:** the architecture is sufficient but the DTEP adapter/compiler produces the wrong state;
- **policy mismatch:** the external decision uses an institution-specific rule that was not represented in the frozen decision policy.

This classification prevents an unavailable document or a domain-specific vocabulary difference from being misreported as evidence against the architecture.
